# Stabilization Research Brief — landmark/pose streams for browser glasses try-on @ 15 fps

## 1. Gaze-invariant head pose

**The gaze-coupling is a confirmed upstream MediaPipe defect, documented since 2021.** [Issue #1786 "Almost all Face Landmarks are (undesirably) affected by eyes and irises motion"](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/issues/1786) matches your live finding exactly. Maintainer kostyaby's diagnosis: landmarks are "much more receptive to intrinsic face motions... such landmarks should be excluded" from pose fitting. Sanctioned workarounds: adjust per-landmark weights in `geometry_pipeline_metadata_landmarks.pbtxt` (he warns it's only a minor improvement), and prefer *adding stable landmarks* over removing unstable ones. Community finding in-thread: **landmarks 2 (under-nose) and 164 (philtrum) are "fairly independent of iris motion"; eyebrow landmarks are "often very unreliable."**

**The facialTransformationMatrix is itself already the "robust rigid subset" pattern.** The [geometry pipeline metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/modules/face_geometry/data/geometry_pipeline_metadata_landmarks.pbtxt) fits the matrix by weighted Procrustes over exactly **34 landmarks** — no eyelids, no irises. Dominant weights: nose wings **129/358 (0.121 each)**, nose tip **4 (0.071)**, jaw **136/365 (0.067)**, eye corners **33/263 (0.059) + 133/362 (0.053)**, cheek 205/425 (0.047), bridge **6 (0.032)**, plus small forehead/temple weights (10, 54/284, 67/297, 127/356, 117/346, 119/348, 121/350, 132/361, 143/372, 147/376, 198/420). This structurally explains your measurement: the matrix stays sub-degree under gaze because eyelid/iris points never enter the fit — but note ~0.22 of total weight sits on eye corners plus 0.03 on bridge-6, so a residual gaze term exists. Sub-degree rotation at the glasses' ~80–100 mm moment arm is ~1–3 px at your scale: near, but not necessarily below, perceptual threshold (see §4).

**Attention Mesh architecture** ([arXiv 2006.10962](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.10962)): eyes/lips come from separate refinement submodels re-inserted into the mesh — the eye *region* (including bridge-adjacent vertices) is optimized for expressive fidelity, not rigidity. There is no upstream fix; consumers work around it by pose-fitting on non-eye subsets or (kostyaby's suggestion) treating gaze as a separate signal entirely.

**Literature pattern matching your leading hypothesis:** [Guo et al., "Towards stabilizing facial landmark detection and tracking via hierarchical filtering"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016003219309561) uses exactly the split you propose: a 3D face model guarantees smoothness of the *global pose trajectory*, while a Kalman filter plus a learned per-landmark quality model handles locals. [Combined detection+salient-feature-tracking frameworks](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/321682625_Combined_Framework_for_Real-Time_Head_Pose_Estimation_using_Facial_Landmark_Detection_and_Salient_Feature_Tracking) similarly derive pose from rigid landmarks + tracked features on a geometric head model, robust at extreme poses.

## 2. Filtering practice beyond One Euro, at 15 fps

- **One Euro at low sample rates**: behavior is sample-rate dependent; the derivative (speed) estimate gets noisy at 15 fps, causing the cutoff itself to jitter. [Casiez's own tuning guidance](https://github.com/casiez/OneEuroFilter): lower `dcutoff` to suppress derivative bursts; tune `mincutoff` on a held-still face with `beta=0`, then raise `beta` only to fix lag. MediaPipe's stream-mode `landmarkSmoothing` is already One Euro — stacking more of the same is why you got whack-a-mole.
- **Double exponential smoothing (LaViola)**: [predictive DES runs ~135× faster than Kalman with equivalent prediction performance](http://cs.brown.edu/~jjl/pubs/kfvsexp_final_laviola.pdf); [DES jitter compensation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0020025512007852) is an established pattern. The **shipped production version is Microsoft's joint filter (Azure Kinect Body Tracking)**: DES + `jitterRadius` (soft dead zone: deviations under the radius are heavily smoothed) + `maxDeviationRadius` (clamp on how far the filtered value may trail the raw one — your "guard cap," formalized) + separate smoothing/correction/prediction gains — [reference implementation](https://gist.github.com/UnaNancyOwen/be527932994adbe639521edb4462b1dd). Critically these are *continuous radial* falloffs, not binary gates, so they don't flap.
- **Adaptive/conditioned Kalman**: making R a function of conditions is standard ([innovation-based R adaptation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.00884), [measurement-noise-adapting KF](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/24/8304), [sample-based adaptive KF for camera pose](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925231218314978)). **No published work found that conditions R specifically on head pitch angle** — your measured 2–6× noise-vs-pitch curve is ahead of the literature; encode it directly as R(pitch) rather than rediscovering it via innovation statistics (which converge too slowly at 15 fps).
- **Learned smoothing+prediction at low fps**: [N-euro Predictor (MobiSys '23)](https://cave.cs.columbia.edu/Statics/publications/pdfs/Shao_Mob23.pdf) shows neural smoothing beats One Euro at low-rate mobile tracking — evidence that hand-tuned filters saturate at low fps. But it's a *prediction* method; your frame-locked constraint removes the need. The transferable lesson: at 15 fps every frame of filter lag is 66 ms, so the winning move is not a better filter on the fast path but **removing landmark noise from the fast path entirely** (pose-only drive), which is your hypothesis.
- **Production web try-on practice**: [Jeeliz's face tracker](https://github.com/jeeliz/jeelizFaceFilter) stabilizes with a floating average whose strength is a **continuous confidence factor k∈[0,1]** — k→0 (responsivity) when moving fast/rotating/poor detection, k→1 (heavy smoothing) when still and confident. A smooth trust blend, never a threshold — the direct answer to your flapping gaze gate.

## 3. "Anchor to transform, re-derive transform robustly"

- Trusting MediaPipe's matrix **is** trusting a fixed-weight Umeyama over the 34-point set above. A custom re-derivation only wins if you change the ingredients: (a) drop/downweight the gaze-tainted members (eye corners 33/133/263/362, bridge 6), (b) add the community-verified gaze-independent points 2 and 164, (c) robustify — [RANSAC+PnP is standard for noisy correspondences](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334641917_Real-Time_Head_Pose_Estimation_by_Tracking_and_Detection_of_Keypoints_and_Facial_Landmarks), and **confidence-weighted Umeyama** (weights from per-point confidence maps) is the modern robust-alignment pattern — your person model's per-vertex W is exactly the right weight source, an asset nothing off-the-shelf has.
- Typical rigid subsets across the literature: nose ridge *excluding tip for expression but tip is fine for gaze* (4 is safe from gaze, wobbly under extreme pitch), nose wings, under-nose/philtrum (2, 164), malar cheeks (117/346, 205/425), temples (127/356, 54/284), jaw corners (136/365), forehead (10, 67/297). Eyebrows: unreliable. Eyelids/irises: never. Common solvePnP tutorials use {1, 33, 263, 61, 291, 199} — too few and eye-corner-heavy; the 34-point weighted set minus eye region is the better starting basis.
- At extreme pitch, per-landmark robust weights help less than fixing the fit's input covariance: MediaPipe's Procrustes weights are pose-independent, so a **pitch-conditioned weighted Umeyama** (your R(pitch) curve as per-landmark down-weighting, e.g. nose region degrades fastest under back-tilt as it foreshortens) can plausibly beat the stock matrix exactly where you observed it failing.

## 4. Perceptual thresholds

- [Wilmott, Erkelens, Murdison & Rio 2022 (Meta Reality Labs, IEEE VR)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9995228/): AR-HMD jitter detection thresholds **~2.5–3.6 arcmin** (2.49 at 60 cd/m², 3.62 at 10 cd/m²); sub-pixel jitter can be perceptible; perceptibility grows with viewing distance and drops with background luminance. Related VR work: ~0.77 arcmin threshold at 12 Hz jitter; [subthreshold jitter still induces discomfort](https://doi.org/10.1145/3746059.3747607) — being "just under detectable" is not fully safe.
- Converted to your setup (laptop screen ~0.2 mm pixels at ~50–60 cm): 1 px ≈ 1.2–1.5 arcmin, so detection threshold ≈ **1.5–2.5 px RMS**. Your measured symptoms (9–28 px gaze swing, 31 px RMS back-tilt) are ~10–20× over threshold. Video-mirror AR is somewhat more forgiving than optical see-through (composited background shares the pipeline), but not an order of magnitude.
- No vendor (FittingBox, Banuba, Jeeliz) publishes numeric jitter targets — marketing-level "stable tracking" only. Derive the ratchet target from psychophysics: **≤2 px RMS at rest and under pure gaze motion**.

## Five most actionable items

1. **Commit to pose-matrix-only drive (rigid-on-converged-person-model).** The pbtxt shows the matrix is already fit only to 34 mostly-rigid points — structural cause of your sub-degree gaze result — and the hierarchical-filtering literature validates the global-pose/local-landmark split. Landmarks feed only the slow A/b/W model; nothing per-frame from landmarks touches the screen.
2. **Budget the residual: sub-degree ≈ 1–3 px at your moment arm.** If live tests show the stock matrix still carries visible gaze wobble or extreme-pitch jiggle, re-derive the transform yourself: weighted Umeyama over the 34-point basis **minus 33/133/263/362 and 6, plus 2 and 164**, Huber-weighted residuals, weights multiplied by person-model per-vertex confidence and by your measured R(pitch) curve.
3. **Replace the binary gaze gate with a continuous trust factor (Jeeliz k-pattern, Kinect jitterRadius pattern).** Smoothing strength = smooth monotone function of (gaze delta, pitch, innovation); no thresholds anywhere on the fast path — thresholds are why eye circles flap at 27% frozen.
4. **Filter the 6-DoF pose (quaternion-log + translation), not landmarks/pixels, with Kinect-style DES**: soft jitter radius + max-deviation clamp, One Euro only with `dcutoff` lowered for 15 fps and still-face-tuned `mincutoff`. One filter, one place, after the transform is derived.
5. **Set the harness ratchet from psychophysics: ≤2 px RMS at rest and during eye circles** (detection threshold at your geometry); at extreme pitch degrade gracefully by stiffening the pose filter with the measured R(pitch) — a stiff-but-attached frame at 49° pitch is acceptable; a 31 px jiggle is not.
