# How production face-AR systems keep glasses stable — research brief (2026-08-17)

## 1. Per-system anchoring models

| System | What carries the glasses frame-to-frame | Landmark role | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Google MediaPipe Face Effect (official glasses example)** | The **face pose transformation matrix only** — a rigid Procrustes fit of the canonical model to the runtime mesh; glasses are a static child of that transform. Face mesh is rendered separately as a depth-buffer occluder. | Landmarks feed the Procrustes solve as a **33-point weighted basis**, never drive the object directly | [MediaPipe 3D Face Transform blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/mediapipe-3d-face-transform/), [geometry_pipeline_metadata_landmarks.pbtxt](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/mediapipe/modules/face_geometry/data/geometry_pipeline_metadata_landmarks.pbtxt) |
| **Snap Lens Studio (glasses template)** | A **Head Binding** — object parented to a tracked head transform at a named attachment point (Head Center, Forehead, etc.); occluder is a separate head mesh | Attachment point is a fixed offset in head space; objects do not follow individual landmarks live | [Head Attached 3D Objects docs](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/ar-tracking/face/head-attached-3d-objects) |
| **Jeeliz FaceFilter / GlassesVTOWidget** (open source, ran a commercial eyewear VTO) | A **single 6-DoF pose straight from the neural net** (`x, y, s, rx, ry, rz` per face) — no landmark following at all; `JeelizThreeHelper` converts that one pose to a three.js transform with a fixed pivot offset | No landmarks exist in the pipeline; stability comes from the tracker plus detection hysteresis (`detectionThreshold`/`detectionHysteresis`) and `set_stabilizationSettings()` | [JeelizThreeHelper.js](https://github.com/jeeliz/jeelizFaceFilter/blob/master/helpers/JeelizThreeHelper.js), [jeelizGlassesVTOWidget](https://github.com/jeeliz/jeelizGlassesVTOWidget) |
| **WebAR.rocks.face** (Jeeliz's successor; powers current accessories/glasses VTO demos) | **solvePnP over a 6-landmark rigid subset** — `chin, leftEarBottom, rightEarBottom, noseOuter, leftEyeExt, rightEyeExt` — after per-landmark One Euro stabilization; the resulting single pose carries the object | Landmarks are filtered first (One Euro, `minCutOff 0.001`, `beta 50`, divergence-reset blending), THEN reduced to one rigid pose; deformable regions (eyelids, mouth) are excluded from the pose solve | [WebARRocksFaceThreeHelper.js](https://github.com/WebAR-rocks/WebAR.rocks.face/blob/master/helpers/WebARRocksFaceThreeHelper.js), [OneEuroLMStabilizer.js](https://github.com/WebAR-rocks/WebAR.rocks.face/blob/master/helpers/landmarksStabilizers/OneEuroLMStabilizer.js), [README stabilization notes](https://github.com/WebAR-rocks/WebAR.rocks.face) |
| **Apple ARKit** | `ARFaceAnchor.transform` — a rigid anchor transform; deformation is delivered separately as 52 blendshape coefficients, so expression motion is structurally divorced from the anchor | Glasses attach to the anchor; blendshapes never move it | [ARFaceAnchor blendshapes docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor/blendshapelocation), [Creating Face-Based AR Experiences](https://github.com/DaoKeLegend/Creating-Face-Based-AR-Experiences/blob/master/README.md) |
| **8th Wall Face Effects** | `FaceAttachment` component: entity transform follows a named attachment point of the tracked face (forehead, eyebrow, etc.) — a head-transform + offset model | Named points, not raw per-frame landmark following | [FaceAttachment API](https://www.8thwall.com/docs/api/studio/ecs/face-attachment/), [three.js glasses example](https://www.8thwall.com/8thwall/face-effects-threejs) |
| **Banuba** | Proprietary "Face Kernel": infers a **3D head model directly** from video with no intermediate 2D-landmark step; markets stability at extreme angles as the differentiator vs 2D-point SDKs | No public landmark pipeline | [Banuba vs DeepAR comparison](https://www.banuba.com/blog/banuba-face-ar-sdk-vs-deepar-tested-feature-comparison), [Banuba technology page](https://www.banuba.com/technology/) |
| **FittingBox** (eyewear specialist, 59 patents) | Landmark detection (eye corners, nose bridge, cheek contours) fused into a **head pose** that anchors the frame; fit-once-then-track | Public material confirms pose-anchoring, not live landmark following | [FittingBox how-it-works](https://fittingbox.com/en/resources/blog/how-3d-glasses-simulators-work-the-technology-behind-virtual-try-on), [FAQ](https://fittingbox.com/en/resources/faq-eyewear-virtual-try-on-technology) |
| **Ditto** (eyewear specialist) | Sidesteps live tracking entirely: one-time multi-angle **face scan** (~900 points, credit-card scale reference) builds a personal 3D head; glasses are composited onto the replayed scan, not a live mirror | N/A — the "person model, then rigid" idea taken to its extreme | [SmartBuyGlasses Ditto explainer](https://www.smartbuyglasses.co.uk/blog/all-eyes-on/introducing-ditto-virtual-3d-try-feature-eyewear-2.html), [7x7 profile](https://www.7x7.com/dittos-virtual-try-on-technology-enables-you-to-buy-eyeglasses-online-1781771918.html) |

**Bottom line for Q1: no production system follows individual landmarks live.** Every one collapses measurement into a single rigid head transform (learned, PnP-solved, or Procrustes-fit) and parents the glasses to it; occlusion is a separate head-mesh depth pass.

## 2. The gaze-coupling is a known, documented MediaPipe defect

- **[Issue #1786 "Almost all Face Landmarks are (undesirably) affected by eyes and irises motion"](https://github.com/google/mediapipe/issues/1786)** — reports exactly your finding: most landmarks move when only the irises move, corrupting the face pose transformation matrix. Closed as legacy without a fix; the explicit ask ("make the matrix not depend on iris/eye motion") was never implemented.
- Mechanism consistent with the [Attention Mesh architecture](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.10962): eye/lip region submodels re-predict landmarks from attention crops, and `refineLandmarks` shifts positions of the base 468 — eye-region state bleeds into nearby geometry (bridge/nose).
- **The matrix is structurally more gaze-resistant than any single landmark**, and now you know exactly why: the Procrustes fit uses a fixed **33-landmark weighted basis** ([metadata file](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/mediapipe/modules/face_geometry/data/geometry_pipeline_metadata_landmarks.pbtxt)) with weights chosen "to distinguish a head pose change from a facial expression change" ([Google blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/mediapipe-3d-face-transform/)). Heaviest weights: nose sides 129/358 (0.121 each), nose tip 4 (0.071), jaw 136/365 (0.067), outer eye corners 33/263 (0.059), inner eye corners 133/362 (0.053), cheeks 205/425 (0.047), bridge 6 (0.032). **No eyelid, iris, or mouth landmarks are in the basis**, and gaze-coupled bridge error is diluted across 33 points and 6 DoF — matching your measured sub-degree matrix response while the raw bridge landmark swings 2-4 mm.
- General landmark jitter (static face, static camera) is also long-documented: [#825](https://github.com/google/mediapipe/issues/825), [#3926](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/issues/3926), [#4130](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/issues/4130), [#3495](https://github.com/google/mediapipe/issues/3495) — community consensus mitigation is external filtering (One Euro / Kalman), and MediaPipe itself ships a [`LandmarksSmoothingCalculator` with a One Euro filter](https://developers.google.com/mediapipe/api/solutions/python/mp/calculators/util/landmarks_smoothing_calculator_pb2/LandmarksSmoothingCalculatorOptions/OneEuroFilter) (adaptive: strong smoothing at rest, pass-through under fast motion).

## 3. Extreme-pose behavior in webcam stacks

- MediaPipe face landmarking degrades sharply past ~45° rotation (training-data limitation, acknowledged in GitHub issues; see [#4130](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/issues/4130) thread and [Warudo's MediaPipe docs](https://docs.warudo.app/docs/mocap/mediapipe)). Nobody fixes it downstream — they **gate and interpolate**.
- [Kalidokit](https://github.com/yeemachine/kalidokit) (vtuber standard): `Face.stabilizeBlink(..., {maxRot: 0.5})` — beyond ~0.5 rad head rotation it stops trusting per-eye data and interpolates the obscured eye from the visible one; plus global lerp/easing on everything. Pattern: **derived quantities are replaced by model priors when the pose makes the measurement unreliable**, with hysteresis, not thresholded freezing.
- Live2D/VTuber motion-capture papers layer Kalman/EAR smoothing on MediaPipe output ([Authorea Live2D smoothing study](https://www.authorea.com/doi/full/10.22541/au.169272418.88758655/v1)).
- Jeeliz handles marginal tracking with **detection-confidence hysteresis** (two thresholds, enter/exit) rather than a binary gate ([JeelizThreeHelper](https://github.com/jeeliz/jeelizFaceFilter/blob/master/helpers/JeelizThreeHelper.js)).

## 4. Stable-subset rigid pose estimation is standard practice

- WebAR.rocks (above) is the cleanest in-production example: 6 rigid points → solvePnP, eyelids/mouth excluded.
- Classic OpenCV/dlib head pose: 6 points (nose tip, chin, eye outer corners, mouth corners) into solvePnP ([LearnOpenCV](https://learnopencv.com/head-pose-estimation-using-opencv-and-dlib/)); research variants explicitly pick landmarks "less prone to being affected by facial expressions" — eye corners, nostrils, nose tip ([survey](https://people.ict.usc.edu/~gratch/CSCI534/Old-Readings/Head%20Pose%20estimation.pdf), [salient-points 6-DoF paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/321682530_Real-Time_Monocular_6-DoF_Head_Pose_Estimation_from_Salient_2D_Points)); solvePnPRansac for outlier rejection on larger sets.
- MediaPipe's own 33-point weighted Procrustes basis is the same idea, shipped at scale.

## The 5 most actionable transferable patterns

1. **One rigid transform carries the glasses; landmarks only ever elect it.** Universal across Snap, ARKit, 8th Wall, Google's own glasses example, Jeeliz, WebAR.rocks, FittingBox. Your leading hypothesis (pose matrix / rigid seat on the converged person model, landmarks feeding only the slow model) is exactly the industry architecture — and your pre-rework rejection doesn't apply because the 12-15 mm miss was rigid-on-*canonical*; Ditto proves rigid-on-*personal-scan* is the eyewear-specialist endgame.
2. **Solve your own pose from your own rigid subset instead of trusting either the raw matrix or any single landmark.** You hold a converged person model (better 3D object points than MediaPipe's canonical) — run Umeyama/Kabsch or solvePnP(Ransac) per frame over gaze-immune points only. Start from the intersection of MediaPipe's Procrustes basis and WebAR.rocks' choice: outer/inner eye corners, cheeks, jaw/ear-line, chin — and, given your measurement that the bridge/nose region is gaze-coupled, *drop or down-weight 4/6/129/358 despite MediaPipe weighting them highest*. That makes the carrying transform structurally gaze-proof rather than statistically diluted.
3. **Filter before the pose solve, adaptively (One Euro), never gate binarily.** WebAR.rocks: per-landmark One Euro (minCutOff 0.001, beta 50) with a divergence-reset blend so lag can't build up; MediaPipe ships the same filter internally. One Euro's speed-adaptive cutoff is precisely the anti-whack-a-mole device: strong smoothing at rest (kills gaze residue and 15 fps noise), automatic pass-through under real head motion — no threshold to flap around. Your gaze gate's flapping is the textbook failure One Euro exists to replace.
4. **Condition on pose reliability with hysteresis, substituting priors — don't freeze.** At >30-45° pitch/yaw everyone's detector degrades; vtuber stacks (Kalidokit maxRot) swap unreliable measurements for model-derived values and interpolate back smoothly. For you: as pitch grows, ramp the pose solve's trust toward the person-model prior / last-good-pose prediction (continuous blend keyed on pitch with enter/exit hysteresis), so the 31 px jiggle decays to the smoothed pose instead of oscillating across a tripwire.
5. **Separate deformation from anchoring completely (ARKit's contract), and let the occluder ride the same rigid transform.** Expression/eye state must have no path into the glasses transform — deliver it (if ever needed) as a parallel channel, ARKit-blendshape style. Your seat/occluder-are-one-surface invariant slots in unchanged: seat once on the converged person model in face space, then everything — frame, seat, occluder — moves only with the elected rigid pose. This is also Ditto's lesson: the more the product leans on a converged personal model, the less the live detector is allowed to touch the screen.
