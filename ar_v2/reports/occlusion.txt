OCCLUSION — WHAT THE SCANNED FACE HIDES, MEASURED BEFORE A PIXEL IS DRAWN
==========================================================================
The renderer about to be built will use the scanned face mesh as its
occluder. This instrument measures, against synthetic ground truth, how
good that occluder is: boundary placement in the glasses band, depth-
ordering flips in both directions, stability under live tracking, and
what the personal scan buys over the average face.

Occluder scans: 12 subjects enrolled at eye-level, framesPerBeat 12, true iris. Frame: 'standard'
seated by solveSeat on the scan (the shipping path). The frame sample
cloud samples all five parts the renderer draws — rims, lens discs,
bridge, endpieces, temples — off the SAME `fit/frame-layout.ts` the
renderer builds from, so this is render geometry and not a second
description of it. The line that stood here said "rims, bridge and
temples ... not render geometry", and both halves had become false:
two of the five parts were unsampled, and the bridge samples sat
4.000000 mm above the drawn tube — 2.4 mm clear of a 1.6 mm radius,
so nothing they reported was about geometry that existed. Every
occluder is posed
by PnP against noiseless truth landmarks (what the tracker does, minus
its noise — metric C carries the noise) and depth-gauge normalised, so
a ruler error cannot masquerade as an occlusion error.

Campaign: seeds {11, 23, 37, 41, 53}. Seed 11 runs the full population (12 subjects: 10 sampled + 2 named extremes) and provides every table below; the four replication seeds run 6 subjects (4 sampled + the extremes) — the population was shrunk before the yaw ladder, per the wall-clock rule, and the headline block reports all five.

  scan px      is in the geometry's NATIVE pixels: a phone pixel is a

               a compositor cares about.
  a 0.00 med   is a statement, not a rounding: more than half the band
               boundary coincides to within one buffer cell (~0.7-0.9 mm,
               ~1 native px at eye level), which is the grid the per-
               pixel distances are quantised to.
  off          the mean boundary OFFSET: in-band area where the two
               masks disagree, over the truth contour's in-band length
               (median across subjects). Sub-cell-resolving, and robust
               where the raw distances are not: a knife-edge sliver of
               face exposed in one mask only reads as its own thin area
               here, while the nearest-distance walks its arc and can
               report tens of millimetres for a sub-mm shape difference
               (that tail is real and lives in the p90 columns).
  scan buys    template offset minus scan offset, per subject, median.
               Metric D's headline: the boundary error the average face
               would add if it were the occluder.

  gauge err    how far the pooled arm's depth gauge sat from truth before
               normalisation — the ruler error the pose absorbed. That
               these percents dwarf the |delta| columns is the point: the
               ruler moves the gauge, the gauge cancels in projection, and
               the boundary does not notice. A |delta| of 0.00 here is
               EXACT, not merely small: the ruler multiplies the solved
               shape globally (applyScale in enroll.ts), so the pooled and
               true-iris models differ by a pure similarity that the PnP
               fit and the gauge normalisation cancel to the last bit.
               Scale provably cannot reach occlusion in this pipeline.
               (The pooled arm is measured against the SCAN arm's band
               so only the surface differs.)

METRIC B — DEPTH-ORDERING FLIPS, AND THE BIAS TRADE
---------------------------------------------------
Every frame sample is classified hidden/visible against the truth depth
buffer and against the scanned one (hidden = face nearer by more than
0.5 mm). Contested = hidden by either surface at that bias; the
fractions are of contested samples. X-RAY (truth-hidden, scan-visible)
is the failure the field never tolerates; FORGIVEN (truth-visible,
scan-hidden) it forgives. Sign convention: NEGATIVE bias moves the
occluder TOWARD the camera, hides more, and buys X-ray down at the
price of forgiven — pinned by its own test in pipeline.test.ts.

  The temple arm grazes the head, so its samples sit within a
  millimetre of the surface for most of their length — precisely the
  geometry that flips on sub-millimetre shape error, in either
  direction. A bias spent to hide temple X-ray is a bias that starts
  eating the rim, and this table is where that trade is visible.

How deep the X-ray really is: an X-rayed sample sat a median 3.33 mm
behind the truth surface (p90 16.86, worst 78.2, over 1886 flips at
yaw >= 30). Two populations live in that spread. The shallow half are
grazing skims — the temple riding within a couple of millimetres of
the cheek. The deep tail is samples far behind the head exposed at an
occluding EDGE (the nose profile, the mask's own cut edge) whose
position the scan has sub-millimetre wrong: the flip is deep but the
wrongly-drawn strip is only as wide as metric A's boundary offset.
Note the rim row above: its X-ray is IDENTICAL at bias 0 and -0.5,
because those samples land on pixels the scan mask does not cover at
all — no depth bias can hide a sample the occluder has no depth for.
A bias fixes depth-ordering X-ray only; edge-coverage X-ray is fixed
by boundary accuracy (metric A) or by the renderer dilating its
mask edge, which is a stage-1 decision this table informs.

METRIC C — STABILITY UNDER LIVE TRACKING
----------------------------------------
A 40-frame wandering hold near 35 degrees of yaw — the capture
machinery's own postural wander and landmark noise — tracked with the
real tracker against the scan. Per frame: banded boundary error of the
scan at the TRACKED pose against truth at the TRUE pose. The crawl is
the median frame-to-frame change of that error — the wobble the eye
punishes. The shape-only arm re-poses the same scan with noiseless
landmarks, so tracked minus shape-only is what tracking itself costs.

----------  ------  ----  --------------  --------------  ---------  -------------
eye-level      480     0            0.55           0.085      0.176           0.31
laptop-lid     480     0            0.45           0.079      0.114           0.31
phone-lap      480     0            0.57           0.050      0.071           0.23

  A lost frame is one the tracker refused (solve rejected or held); the
  boundary numbers are conditioned on tracking. This legend used to go on
  to call the phone-lap loss rate "a finding of its own" -- 35 degrees of
  yaw plus the ~30 degree pitch a phone in the lap imposes -- while the
  table above it printed lost 0 on all three cameras. Whatever produced
  that reading, this instrument no longer reproduces it, so the claim is
  gone rather than restated; the column is the place to look.

READING THIS TABLE
------------------
The scan mm column is the error a wearer would see at the frame's edge
where it crosses the face contour: how many millimetres of temple arm
vanish too early or too late. The buys column is the same number for the
average head, minus the scan's — the argument for having scanned at all,
and it should GROW with yaw, because frontal occlusion is nearly shape-
free while profile occlusion is nothing but shape. X-ray percent is the
one to watch in metric B: a single X-rayed temple sample is a temple
drawn through a cheek. The crawl is metric C's verdict on the frozen-
scan architecture: the scan does not change between frames, so boundary
wobble can only come from pose noise, and it should sit well under a
native pixel-equivalent (~0.3-0.9 mm at these distances).

A limitation, stated: truth here is the same 468-vertex mask topology
as the occluder, so everything the mask can never cover — ears, hair,
the back of the head — is outside this instrument by construction.
Whether the renderer should hide a temple tip behind hair it has no
geometry for is a stage-1 renderer question, not a scan-quality
question, and no number above speaks to it.
