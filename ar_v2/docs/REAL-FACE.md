# The first real face

**2026-08-26. Two scans of one wearer, an hour apart, on the same webcam
(USB2.0 FHD UVC, 3277:0055), replayed through the shipping estimator.**

Until this file existed, every accuracy figure in this repository was synthetic —
drawn from a population generated by the same shape basis the estimator fits,
with no expression change, no ageing, no relatives and no glasses already on the
face. That was the largest thing not known about this system.

It is known now, for one face.

## The two captures

| | A | B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| frames | 141 | 165 |
| turn achieved | 44° | 48° |
| beats recorded | `centre` **1** | `centre` **4** |
| solved focal length | 1156.5 px | 1027.2 px |
| reprojection rms | 5.97 px | 5.96 px |
| variance factor | 2.97 | 2.19 |
| scale rung | iris ×1.9225 ±5.12% | iris ×2.1021 ±4.95% |
| PD readout | 64.2 ±3.3 mm | 67.5 ±3.3 mm |
| nose sigma | 0.648 mm | 0.625 mm |
| degraded | no | no |
| coverage | sufficient, profile, intrinsics solvable | same |

Both solve in about 600 ms, both are undegraded, both reach a profile, and all
ten catalogue frames seat and converge on each.

## The result that matters: it is CALIBRATED

Two scans of one face differ by **4.167 mm rms** across all 468 vertices. Almost
all of that is one number: remove the best rotation, uniform scale and
translation between them and **0.828 mm rms** is left. The scale it removes is
**4.9%**.

So the disagreement is a size disagreement, not a shape disagreement — and the
system says so before being asked. Per region, after the similarity is removed,
against the model's own claimed per-vertex sigma:

| region | vertices | repeat rms | repeat p90 | **claimed sigma** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **nose** | 133 | **0.397 mm** | 0.684 | **0.734 mm** |
| padStrip | 20 | 0.415 | 0.550 | 0.797 |
| bridge | 13 | 0.403 | 0.549 | 0.672 |
| eyes | 124 | 0.458 | 0.684 | 0.590 |
| temples | 12 | 0.568 | 0.747 | 6.981 |
| cheeks | 14 | 0.802 | 1.327 | 7.109 |

**Every region repeats inside its own claimed uncertainty**, and the same holds
for scale: the model reported ±5.12% and ±4.95%, and the two scans differ by
4.9%. Nothing here is over-claimed, in either direction, on either quantity.

That is the hardest property in the system to get right and it is the one this
measurement confirms. An estimator that is imprecise and says so is useful; an
estimator that is precise and cannot prove it is not.

**The nose is the sharpest result**, because the nose is what the whole v2
rebuild was for: 0.397 mm rms between two independent scans, against a claimed
0.734. The free-form displacement field is finding the same nose twice.

## The weakness, and its fix

Absolute size is the one thing that does not repeat: **5.2%** between the two
scans, measured on temple width. Both ran on the **iris** rung, because
`knownPdMm` was null in both headers — the wearer never set a PD.

Supplying one collapses it:

| ruler | A temple width | B temple width | A vs B | claimed sigma |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| iris (as captured) | 158.14 mm | 166.38 mm | **+5.21%** | ~5.0% |
| PD 63 mm | 155.28 | 155.28 | **0.00%** | 0.79% |
| PD 64 mm | 157.74 | 157.74 | **0.00%** | 0.78% |
| PD 67 mm | 165.14 | 165.14 | **0.00%** | 0.75% |

**Read that honestly.** The 0.00% is repeatability, not accuracy: the PD rung
rescales the geometry so `interpupillarySpan` equals the typed number by
construction, so two scans agree exactly and both are wrong by however wrong the
typed number is. `docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` already records that the PD rung's
confidence moves the wrong way for this reason. What the table does establish is
that **the 5.2% is entirely the iris rung**, and that the machinery downstream of
the ruler is exact.

`ScaleEstimate.disagreementPct` — the second-ruler check — earns its place here.
It reports how far the iris reading sat from the typed PD, and the two scans
disagree: at PD 64 it reads **+0.3%** on A and **+5.5%** on B. So the two
captures' iris reads differ, one of them materially, and the check says which.
This is the first time that field has run on a real wearer with two rulers.

## What this does NOT establish

- **One face.** Two scans of one person on one camera. It says the estimator is
  self-consistent and honestly calibrated on this wearer; it says nothing about
  a population, and nothing about a face far from the template.
- **No ground truth.** Nobody measured this face with calipers, so "repeatable
  to 0.4 mm" is not "accurate to 0.4 mm". Repeatability bounds precision, not
  bias — a systematic error present in both scans is invisible here by
  construction.
- **The solved focal length moved 11.2%** between two scans on the SAME camera
  (1156.5 → 1027.2 px). `docs/SCALE.md` already records that
  `model.intrinsics.f` is not a physical focal length and is accurate only in
  combination with the solved depth; this is the first real-face confirmation,
  and it is the mechanism behind the 5% scale spread.
- **The `centre` beat is thin**: 1 frame of 141 in A, 4 of 165 in B. Centre
  establishes the neutral every later beat is measured against. It did not
  degrade either scan, but nobody designed it that way.

## Two defects this confirmed in production

The captures make both production defects facts rather than inferences.

- **`silhouette` is null on 100% of real frames** (0 of 141, 0 of 165), while
  `useSilhouette` defaults true. Every silhouette gate in `bundle.ts` skips on
  every real scan, so the harness's `no-silhouette` variant *is* the production
  configuration and every published enrolment figure was measured on the other
  arm.
- **`visibility` is present on 100% of real frames** (141 of 141, 165 of 165),
  which is `collectFrame` overwriting the deliberate `null` with `fill(1)`.

## How to reproduce

The captures are **not committed**, and that is deliberate: they are 478-point
facial landmark streams of a named person, and `docs/PRIVACY.md` records that
v1's equivalent fixtures were committed against its own README's stated policy —
35 MB of one person's facial geometry in a public repository. Deleting those was
an improvement and re-creating them here would undo it.

They live in the owner's own `Downloads`. To re-run:

```bash
node scripts/replay-capture.mjs "<path to capture.ndjson>"
```

The numbers above are the record. If a future change moves them, that is the
finding.

## 2026-09-04 — the first WEAR recording, and the first replay of one

Everything above this line is enrollment: a scan, solved again offline. The half
a wearer actually watches — the tracker, the One Euro, the motion prior — had no
recorded input at all until this date, which is why every decision about it had
been made on a synthetic stimulus or on a spoken report of a session nobody
could run again.

`enroll/telemetry.ts` now carries a `wear` section (landmarks, `dt`, and the two
poses the app produced), `app/main.ts` keeps a rolling 900-frame window, and
`scripts/replay-capture.mjs` drives it back through the shipping tracker.

**The first capture.** USB webcam, 1280x720, f solved at 933.1, no PD entered
(so the iris rung). 227 scan frames, 900 wear frames — about thirty seconds of a
wearer holding still, turning slowly, turning fast, and holding still again.

**The replay is faithful.** Of 885 frames after a 15-frame warm-up, the emitted
pose reproduces the session to **3.5e-3 mm median**, within 0.01 mm on 86% of
frames, 0.1 mm on 98.1% and 1 mm on **100%**. The floor is landmark
quantisation: perturbing the recorded landmarks by half their stored precision
moves the emitted pose by up to 0.029 mm, which is the same order as the
residual gap, and wear frames now record an extra decimal because of it.

**The 15 excluded frames are a property of the format, not a defect.** The
window is rolling, so it opens mid-session with the app's tracker already warm —
a previous pose, a smoother with history, a velocity — and a replay starts cold.
The worst warm-up frame is 3.162 mm and the gap is gone by frame 7. A recorder
that wanted those frames would have to serialise the tracker's state, and it is
not worth it: nothing is measured on the first half-second.

**What it settled.** See Q1 for the detector's landmark noise (1.19 source px on
quiet frames, against 0.7 assumed) and Q7 for what that does to the filter
verdict. Two harness constants that had never been measured now have a number:
`CAPTURE_DEFAULTS.noisePx` is 1.7x optimistic, and `CaptureOptions.wanderScale`
should be about 0.14 — this wearer's bridge moves 0.181 mm/frame while holding
still, where the harness's moves 1.328.

**What it did not settle.** Scale, because no PD was entered. One capture, one
camera, one face, one room's lighting — every figure above is n=1 and should be
read as an order of magnitude with a direction, not a constant. The capture
itself is not committed and must not be: it is a 478-point landmark stream of a
named person. See `docs/PRIVACY.md`.
