# End-to-end integration status

2026-09-21. A resumable photo-input/initializer/refinement/region-observation job flow and bounded optical-layer rendering now exist. Verified semantic identity, optical inference and general optical-surface preparation remain unfinished; this is not a validated end-to-end automation or deployment claim.

## Automation entry point

`reconstruction/job.py` now owns requests with variable photos, optional/repeated angle priors, optional dimensions, immutable originals/normalized copies, source/package hashes and stage results. `input_bundle.py` records EXIF and color transformations without claiming calibration. Initializer, shared refinement and automatic region/color observations are connected, and recovery verifies pinned artifacts before continuing. Region alternatives and authored-height proxies remain conditional evidence; they cannot satisfy semantic or optical quality gates. [The job contract](JOBS.md) describes current executable behavior. Dimensions are preserved but remain unapplied; transparent images need alpha-aware observations. The historical outer controllers impose exactly five photos, required dimensions/layout and obsolete appearance gates, so they are not imported wholesale.

The newer `ar/modeling/modeling/providers/meshy.py` supplied source-level reference for durable provider operations, but its convenience wrapper requires front/back/left/right and ignores angled views. The automation-owned `initializer.py` instead copies verified existing GLBs, imports saved provider artifacts with explicit origin uncertainty, or selects at most four inputs for an injected Meshy backend while retaining all photos for fitting. Submission reservation/task/artifact receipts prevent blindly repeating an uncertain POST. No authenticated HTTP transport or default credential discovery is provided yet. New paid runs need an explicit case list and bounded authorization; saved artifacts suffice for local adapter development.

`ar/modeling/modeling/pipeline/native.py` exposes `NativeRunner.run(...)`. `parts_prepare` accepts split/textured GLBs, layout, dimensions and product metadata; `export_mirror` accepts the prepared master, directory and frame width. Calling preparation without old `front_targets` and `materials` avoids the old front-only fit and appearance heuristics before the new stages.

Preparation is itself a hypothesis generator: it classifies parts, removes inferred backing walls, may join or rebuild lenses, reseats surfaces and fits temple length. Preserve the input GLBs and every fallback. Unknown layout should remain competing hypotheses; an internal normalization width must not be reported as a measured dimension.

After normalization, invoke the shared refinement stage. Attach canonical appearance only when a descriptor and valid optical surface coordinates are supported. The legacy white-background material fit with adjustable environment intensity does not identify separate transmission and reflection. The newer exporter also strips extras and does not establish the required intrinsic lens UVs, so it needs a general semantic/coordinate postprocessor. The current fixture exporter cannot substitute for that postprocessor.

## Production AR consumer

The actual `TryOnRenderer` now installs the canonical material adapter before configuring geometry. Existing uncommitted AR changes, including environment ownership, were preserved and extended.

1. One fetched GLB byte sequence supplies parsing and continuity, with SHA-256 verification against a configured pin and a runtime integrity report. Assets without a pin are explicitly reported as unpinned. The strict shared `isOpticalMaterial` classifier preserves canonical optical identity at zero transmission across renderer, shadow, rear-drop, continuity, terminal-fit, temple clipping/visibility and hair occlusion; untagged assets retain the legacy classification.
2. The opt-in adapter owns an opaque/background target and depth-ordered optical passes. Physical `transmission=0` avoids Three's hidden prepass in this path; the canonical descriptor supplies all optical coefficients. Each layer composes `T * behind + R * environment` in linear RGB. The opaque input preserves ordinary frame tone mapping and leaves camera colors untouched; this is a display-linear AR composition, not global HDR tone mapping after every layer. Fallback tint, absorption and Fresnel are not reapplied. Replaced materials are retained until scene disposal to protect shared texture ownership.
3. Visible response uses intrinsic UV height, transformed normals, view incidence and PMREM reflection roughness. Canonical PMREM generation removes the legacy extra `sigma=0.04` preblur; legacy environment behavior is retained. Environment intensity scales illumination, not the coating's reflectance coefficient.
4. The production face-illumination caster evaluates the same canonical transmission at the shadow light's incidence angle, with normalized light-view normals and intrinsic UV height. Ordered light-space depths stop at opaque geometry; the receiver multiplies only layers before its own depth. Its canonical branch bypasses legacy tint and `(1-F)^2`. Receiver/contact/hair filtering remains an illumination approximation.

The bounded `front_sheet_v1` profile requires explicit lens role/profile metadata, a separate single-material mesh, baked identity transforms within the root, finite authored +Z-facing normals and winding, and UV0 height spanning bottom 0 to top 1. Skinning, morphing, instancing and batching are rejected. Meter units and the bridge/front origin remain runtime conventions. Front-side rendering avoids Three's double-sided backface transmission pass. These checks reject common errors; they do not prove global topology or correct intrinsic coordinates, and they do not support closed volumes, back coatings or refraction.

Triangle-level checks require one single-valued authored `z(x,y)` sheet per mesh and reject coincident overlapping patches across meshes, including different tessellations. Separate sheets may overlap or cross, and per-pixel depth peeling handles order changes with pose. Both camera and light support at most four interfaces per ray, with an extra peel and GPU reduction detecting overflow. Nested optical meshes and opaque children remain visible to their proper passes. Mixed legacy optics and translucent frame materials are explicitly unsupported rather than treated as opaque stops.

This path requires float color targets. It reads one generated overflow flag per camera/light layer pass, without reading camera pixels. Extra buffers, passes and synchronous checks require device and sustained-performance validation before broad AR deployment. Global topology, closed volumes, back coatings, refraction and internal optical reflections remain outside this effective front-sheet model.

## Evidence required before calling the automation complete

The actual `TryOnRenderer` conformance harness includes gradients, high and total mirrors, head roll, grazing angles, known opaque content, structured environments/roughness, independently different left/right descriptors, curved sheets, nested geometry and optical layers. A fully transmitting descriptor also checks preservation of ordinary opaque frame display colors. The ordinary production-page lifecycle/protection smoke passes. [Runtime evidence](LENS_RUNTIME.md) records measured outcomes and limits; these controlled fixtures do not certify arbitrary product surfaces.

Negative controls cover missing UVs, wrong normals, undeclared profiles, instancing, coincident interfaces and capacity overflow, supplemented by unit controls for malformed descriptors, reversed coordinates and mixed material groups. Bind rendered geometry, descriptor and report to the same asset hash, distinguishing configured pins from observed hashes. Preserve shadow/hair/stencil, tracking loss, restart/disposal and legacy asset behavior. A valid optical fixture still does not verify inferred product semantics or materials.

Finally freeze the entire photo-input pipeline and run distinct unseen products, counting missing-input outcomes and manual interventions separately. Five archived designs and local mathematical tests cannot establish that generalization. The job flow and initializer boundary are implemented, but authenticated provider execution, general semantic/coordinate preparation, articulated camera/shape hypotheses and uncalibrated lens appearance inference remain engineering work; they are not tasks to shift onto the user as manual modeling.
