2. Describe the faces before adjusting their depth.

Write facet_edit's object note: name each face, corner and edge; say which photos show it, where the edges end,
which edges are ridges or valleys and why, where faceting joins the plain arm, and what is uncertain. Update the
note when that understanding changes. A note is required on the first edit and whenever facet names, their
boundary/corner membership or the set of node names differs from the latest note's layout, including an older variant.

Compare in this order:
- Each visible face reads as one face, with no invented dividing line and no missing face.
- Each visible edge is in the right place and has the observed crispness. Do not soften an unwanted edge to hide
  it, or erase a required edge because its measured angle is small.
- Depth and ridge/valley direction agree with different-direction evidence and its uncertainty. Use the angle
  comparison below to choose an unresolved visible fold.
- Faceting joins the plain arm where and how the photos show, and the complete outline and bend reach the
  photographed tip. State camera conflicts instead of claiming all projections agree.

A ridge is convex and a valley concave. Two faces share an edge; edges meet at shared corners. A triangular face
ends where its boundary edges cross, not where its brightness disappears or one boundary touches the outline.
One apparent side-view corner can contain two corners at different depths: follow each face's own edges in the
angled view before treating them as one point.

A flat face has one plane. If four or more reliable corners cannot lie on a plane within their errors but the
photos show one uninterrupted face, use warp: one face with straight boundary edges and no internal dividing
line. Do not move a reliable corner merely to force flatness. If a photo shows an internal edge, represent the
separate faces instead. A soft border is for a facet meeting plain arm where the photos show a soft transition;
a boundary between facets is never soft.

Facets are clipped to the outline. Free corners beyond a curved outline can let a face meet the whole curve.
Two corners directly on the outline make a straight facet side between them and can leave plain arm between
that side and the curve. A set_node outline choice of top/bottom moves an outline knot to the corner and can
introduce a bend; none keeps the outline independent. Inspect outline_bends instead of accidentally cutting a
notch to accommodate a corner.

3. Measure named features and check the picks.

A square-on outer-side photo mainly places edges in (d, y), not thickness. Moderate-angle views reveal depth and
depth kinks. Top and end-on views constrain the outermost silhouette, not a hidden valley beneath it. A narrow
strip seen from above or below can be a top or bottom surface; use an angled view to identify its boundary with
the outer face before treating it as another outer facet. Tone helps locate a stable boundary, but alone does not
measure depth or fold direction. Render lighting need not reproduce the photo's brightness.

Use photo_read on the same identifiable corner or edge crossing in different photo directions. For a crossing,
trace both edges in each photo and let the host intersect them. Shallow crossings are uncertain; include a
direction with a steeper crossing where available. Read vertices and pointed ends this way instead of choosing
the end of a bright patch. A single pick on an edge can slide, and a silhouette is not automatically a face
boundary. Inspect each pick sheet: every pick must lie on the intended physical feature.

Read d, y, X and t with pick/camera uncertainty, per-photo residuals, slide-line information and warnings. Conflicting
readings can indicate different features, weak directions or inconsistent cameras; inspect the photos and
diagnostics before choosing a cause. Never move a pick just to make a preferred shape build. Replacing a stored
reading does not update a node using its old snapshot: set that named node again with t mode reading. Null d_mm
and y_mm also use the reading's position.

A typed t is an uncertain input to the joint fit, not exact built thickness. Explain it and bracket unresolved
values. Mode base uses the plain arm's thickness there. Mode solve ignores that node's stored reading: surrounding
face/crease constraints and a weak prior determine its value. Inspect fitted positions and W_FIT_OFF_READING where a stored reading exists,
including movements along d and y.
